# Voice and Structure

## Voice

- Write in the first person when describing work Wei actually did or directed.
- Sound like an engineer thinking in public: direct, curious, practical, and willing to say what did not work.
- Use contractions and natural transitions. A short standalone sentence is welcome when it adds rhythm.
- Prefer concrete nouns and verbs: name the model, command, test count, timeout, branch, or bug when those facts are known.
- Keep claims proportional. A small demo can be useful without being revolutionary.
- Preserve unresolved questions as questions when that is honest.

## Shape

A strong post often follows this loose arc:

1. The question or irritation that started the experiment.
2. The first approach and why it was insufficient.
3. The design boundary that made the system useful.
4. A concrete example, including what failed or needed review.
5. Measurements or evidence.
6. Current limitations and the next question.

Use the arc only when it fits the material. Do not force every post into the same headings.

## What to avoid

- Openings such as "In today's rapidly evolving landscape" or "This article explores."
- Abstract claims before the reader knows what was built.
- A heading and bullet list for every idea.
- Symmetrical "Agent A does X / Agent B does Y" lists when a paragraph is clearer.
- Repeating the conclusion after each section.
- Stock phrases such as "This matters," "The key takeaway," or "It's worth noting" unless the sentence adds new substance.
- Claims that the setup is robust, secure, production-ready, or token-efficient without direct evidence.
- Artificially perfect retrospectives. Keep useful friction: timeouts, mistaken assumptions, review findings, and tradeoffs.

## Final voice pass

Read the draft aloud in your head and ask:

- Could this paragraph belong to any AI tooling post? If yes, replace it with a project-specific fact or remove it.
- Does the author sound certain about something the evidence did not prove? Qualify it.
- Are three adjacent paragraphs the same length or cadence? Vary them naturally.
- Did the piece preserve a real opinion or surprise from the work?
- Can a technical reader distinguish what Pi did, what Codex did, and what the author decided?
